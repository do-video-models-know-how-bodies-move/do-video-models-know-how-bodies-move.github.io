Main findings and contributions

Each finding is supported by a figure from the main paper (or a main-paper table plotted as a figure). The figures are interactive: hover over curves, bars, legend entries or panels to highlight.

1Pretraining determines what is encoded

Image self-distillation is best for static pose, whereas predictive and recurrent video self-supervised encoders carry the most transferable dynamics. Vision–language and action-supervised encoders retain little motion: they preserve under 0.36× of true joint speed, compared with 0.70× for RVM.

2Readout design is critical

Linear probes fail to capture multi-rate joint motion and barely improve on predicting zero velocity, while per-joint cross-attention cuts pose error by over 6 cm. Our Temporal Signed Attention (TSA) head differences features across frames instead of averaging them, giving the lowest mean position and velocity error.

3Decoded velocities are genuine dynamical state

During 300 ms blackouts, a baseline that filters per-frame poses alone overshoots (13.23 cm on RVM), whereas a closed-form Kalman filter that fuses the decoded velocities tracks smoothly (9.92 cm) with calibrated uncertainty. Try the “300 ms blackouts + Kalman filter” checkbox above.
